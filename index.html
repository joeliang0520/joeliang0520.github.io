      I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the University of Toronto, advised by Prof. Scott Sanner in the Data Driven & Decision Making (D3M) Lab, and currently an AI Research Intern at the Thoughtworks AI Lab, where I work on world models for AI assistants. My research asks what an egocentric, user-aligned assistant should remember, how it should use that memory, and how it can work out what the user needs. My current focus is spatial memory: linking what the user says to the physical objects the assistant has seen.

      Before my Ph.D., I received an M.A.Sc. in Information Engineering from the University of Toronto, with a thesis on optimization methods for temporal and predictive clustering, and a B.Math. in Computational Mathematics from the University of Waterloo.

      Themes

      Spatial memory

      Grounding conversations in the physical objects an egocentric assistant has seen.

      SpaC-MEM · In-situ labeling (ACL 2026)

      Long-term conversational memory

      Retrieving and reasoning over what an agent has been told across long interactions.

      Goal-Mem · Segment Trees · Semantic XPath

      User needs and recommendation

      Inferring stated and unstated needs for natural-language recommendation and retrieval.

      Scene labeling · GP-LLM scoring · VOGUE · MA-DPR

      Clustering and optimization

      Earlier work on mixed-integer programming for temporal and predictive clustering.
